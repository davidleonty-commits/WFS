# Bucket definitions, failure modes, and evidence weighting

## The buckets

**RED — the lead genuinely lacked the money or access to it.**
**GREEN — capacity demonstrated, no real affordability problem, blocked by something else.**
**GREY — finances were never referenced at all on the call.**
**BLUE — financially qualified and closed.**
**ORANGE — financially disqualified and closed anyway.**
**NA — not a sales call: internal sync, no-show, voicemail only, or a rebooking with no consultation.**

FDQ (financially disqualified) = RED + ORANGE. That is the number management cares about, because ORANGE leads were also unable to fund the program; they just bought anyway.

## The core RED test

Ask: **if the obstacle vanished right now, could this person pay?**

If yes, it is not RED. RED means they do not have the money and cannot get it. Not that paying is inconvenient, gated on a signature, awkwardly timed, or something they would rather not do.

Do not require the literal phrase "I can't afford it." A circumstance that plainly means no buying power counts. What does not count is the absence of any money topic, which is GREY.

### Genuine RED signals

- volunteered inability, or that the price is too much
- a disclosed figure that cannot cover it: liquid cash, savings, surplus after bills, available credit
- low or damaged credit, maxed cards, heavy debt, collections, bankruptcy, garnishment, repossession
- a lender declined and no later lender approved and funded on that call
- no income, job loss, recently laid off, between jobs
- disability, SSDI, SSI, workers comp, unemployment, pension, or fixed income that cannot carry the payment
- no or depleted savings, broke
- homeless, unstable housing, eviction, foreclosure
- recent incarceration, bail, legal fees, restitution
- the money must genuinely come from a spouse, parent, family member, 401k, IRA, tax refund, bonus, settlement, or a future paycheck they do not yet hold
- cannot afford the monthly payment
- capital genuinely committed elsewhere and not recoverable in time

### GREEN signals

- a strong credit score the lead stated (roughly 700+)
- strong stated income relative to a $330 to $670 monthly payment or an $8,000 lump sum
- liquid cash, savings, or available credit that covers it
- financing approved and clearly serviceable

GREEN includes leads where no application was ever run. An unrun application is a fact about the rep, not the lead's wallet.

A GREEN lead's blocker is something else: wants it in writing, wants to research, comparing options, timing, scope questions, or a spouse conversation about the decision itself.

## The ten failure modes

These have each produced a real misgrade on this report. Check every RED against them.

**1. A legal, court, or administrative restriction on a solvent buyer.** Divorce restrictions, credit freezes, a pending mortgage blocking new credit, needing an attorney or accountant to sign off. If funds exist and only permission is missing, that is a decision gate.

*The canonical case:* a lead said "I'm not allowed to draw out more credit any credit" during a divorce and needed attorney approval for a monthly expense. Both reviewers graded RED. She owned a business covering her bills, held real estate, had financed a car through the business, and framed it as "do I wanna pull that or not pull that." The pay-in-full route was never tested. That is GREEN.

**2. A preference, not an inability.** "I don't want to put it on credit," "I buy everything cash," "I'd rather not touch savings." Choosing not to borrow is not being unable to pay.

**3. Timing or logistics with funds confirmed.** Money clearing in a brokerage, a transfer needing a branch visit, a wallet in the car, being mid-shift. Funds exist; only the clock is in the way.

**4. A negotiating posture from a lead with real capacity.** "That's a lot," "is that the best you can do," "is there a cheaper tier" from someone with strong stated income or credit is haggling.

**5. The rep's assumption recorded as the lead's fact.** A credit score, income figure, or affordability read the rep supplied, guessed, or put in the lead's mouth. Only what the lead stated counts.

**6. An answer to a leading question.** "So money is tight then?" answered "yeah" is weak. Compound questions are worse: "what pays the bills, any of them making money?" answered "not yet" says nothing about income.

**7. Pre-price talk about their past.** Money struggles from earlier ventures, said before the number landed and never revisited, are not about this offer.

**8. A garbled or mangled passage.** Callix mangles figures constantly. "$4.26" is $426/mo, "$8" is $8,000, "$5.50 ish" is a 550 credit score, "$1.45" is $145,000. A mangled line proves nothing; resolve it from the rep's restatement or discard it.

**9. An unrun application, or nothing collected.** Facts about what the rep did.

**10. A lender decline that was superseded.** If a later lender approved and funded on the same call, the earlier decline is not a live constraint.

## The opposite trap

Do not over-correct. When a challenge pass is told to knock rows down, it knocks down about 42% and a neutral judge rejects half. Guard against:

- treating any capacity-adjacent sentence as proof of solvency
- a stated income figure cancelling a live lender decline
- a good credit score cancelling maxed cards
- "I could probably get it together" counted as having it
- a lead haggling from $8,000 down to $3,000 read as capable of $8,000 — they may genuinely be unable to reach the price

**When the two readings conflict: what did the lead volunteer, unprompted, closest to the moment money was actually on the table?**

## Evidence weighting

- **Volunteered statements outweigh answers to leading questions.** Always quote the rep's question before any lead answer you rely on. If you cannot show the preceding question, you cannot use the answer.
- **The rep narrowing a lead's objection afterward does not narrow it.** Lead says "I can't do it," rep says "yeah, in one go" — the lead did not scope it to the lump sum.
- **Lump sum versus monthly.** A lead who cannot pay $8,000 today but confirmed they can carry $400 to $600 a month, on credit that supports it, is not RED. But if the monthly was only floated and never affirmed, or they re-opened doubt later, that is RED.
- **Partial payments are not automatically a conversion.** A deposit or spot-hold with financing unfunded and access withheld is not a close; that points RED, not ORANGE.

## Tie-breaks

Only after a full read, when genuinely balanced:
- RED over GREY when a financial reference points at a problem
- GREEN over GREY when capacity is genuinely evident
- ORANGE over BLUE when there is any financial signal
